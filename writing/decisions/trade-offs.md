# Trade-offs

Status: approved · locked · under Key design decisions · not in live case study yet

## Format

I chose [Solution A] over [Solution B] because [reason]. The trade-off I accepted was [what I lost / risked]. That was acceptable because [user or business justification].

## Final (site copy) — locked lead

I chose **refer slide-ups after peak moments in play** over **a streak-revoke referral feature** because survey data showed people do not miss streaks very often. The trade-off I accepted was a possible hit to how many people start a new game right away. That was acceptable because the metric is referrals per person per month, core play is a guardrail that must not get worse, and the ask only appears about once a week.

### Highlight spans

1. `streak-revoke referral feature`
2. `do not miss streaks very often`
3. `start a new game right away`

## Additional trade-offs (approved draft, not required on page)

I chose **putting refer in daily flow** over **leaving it in Settings for people to find** because interviews showed the feature was hard to notice in normal play. The trade-off I accepted was interrupting a celebration or clear with a refer ask. That was acceptable because few people were using the tracked path from Settings, and casual conversation was already winning by default.

I chose **a light non-math first-game nudge for referred players** over **a full referred walkthrough or leading with classic math play** because friends often cooled off as soon as they heard "math game." The trade-off I accepted was one less "pure Matiks" first session for those users. That was acceptable because the existing tutorial already covers onboarding, and the goal was a try before the math label killed interest.

I chose **a once-a-week cap across excite situations** over **prompting on every close win, record, or streak** because stacking asks would feel like spam. The trade-off I accepted was fewer refer chances per month. That was acceptable because an ignored prompt every session would train people to dismiss the feature.

I chose **refer in play** over **browser trial, Play Store trial, or a Hyrox-like referral-ticket tournament** because those died on the platform or turned referral into a whole new product. The trade-off I accepted was not solving try-before-install or event acquisition in this project. That was acceptable because the problem to fix was tracked share in the moment.

### Optional (if 5-minute return nudge is locked)

I chose **a return nudge within about five minutes of leaving to share** over **leaving people in the share sheet with no pull-back** because the refer loop often breaks when someone switches apps. The trade-off I accepted was an interrupt after they left. That was acceptable if it brings them back to finish send without redesigning WhatsApp or the Play Store.
